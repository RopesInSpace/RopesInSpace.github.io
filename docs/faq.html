FAQ

How do I buy a ticket?

Our usual Peer Share, Open Tying and 101 events are not ticketed, you just need to DM us for the address. We ask for a suggested donation of £10 to cover the cost of renting the hall.

Donations can be made in cash on the day, via our bank account (DM or get details on the day) or anonymously via our Open Collective platform.

You can also see all of our income and outgoings and donate to any specific fundraising we are doing outside of the need to pay our rent on our OC platform.

I can’t afford the suggested donation, is that okay?

We don’t check who has donated or how much and we never want anyone to have to choose between attending RiS and meeting their basic needs.

In general we expect a donation from anyone who is in stable employment, has secure housing and is able to cover their household bills each month with some money left over for leisure spending.

If you are able to pay for holidays, education as entertainment or own your own home please consider donating a higher amount to help support us to keep RiS financially accessible for anyone.

If you are able to put aside money in long term savings each month (ie not just saving for a specific purchase), have any investments or own a business or property that generates profit without your direct labour, or charge rent to anyone for a property you own it is expected that you contribute a higher amount than our recommended donation.

Is it okay if I arrive late?

We start setting up the hall at 6.30pm and encourage everyone to help out if they are able. We try to start an opening circle by 6.50pm and it is recommended to attend these at least your first few times at RiS. If for some reason you aren’t able to make it on time for the opening circle please speak to a rainbow lanyarded volunteer so they can make sure you have the important safety and orientation information! I have a disability, can I still come?

We have welcomed attendees with a range of disabilities over the course of our running. Please let us know if there is anything in particular you might need that is not covered below.

We recognise that conflicting access needs are not uncommon and that we may not be able to make the space perfectly accessible for everybody but we wish to be as supportive and transparent as possible about what we can do.

Our venue is fully wheelchair accessible, there is a lift from the ground floor to our main hall on the first floor and there are two accessible toilets on the same level. The fire safety regulations of the building state that in case of a fire wheelchair users who are not able to escape via the stairs should remain in the building until the fire and rescue service arrives.

We are mad and neurodivergent positive in our space and welcome attendees who might display unexpected behaviour such as stimming and vocal tics.

There is a quiet room located near our main hall, though this is communal to all users of the building and cannot be guaranteed to be empty. This is not an appropriate space for tying.

Our lighting is usually moderate and we play background music at a low level at most of our events.

We meet in a gym hall and as such the noise levels, especially overlapping sounds, can get relatively high due to the acoustics but we do try to keep this to a minimum.

People who are hard of hearing or have auditory processing struggles may find it difficult to follow taught classes due to the acoustics of the room but we will do our best to work with you to mitigate this if you let us know you need that support.

We have a DNO air purifier running near the centre of the room during all our events as well as hypochlorous acid sanitiser available for hands and hard surfaces. Our full Infectious Illness Policy is available here attendees are expected to stay away if they are exhibiting any symptoms.

There are chairs with back support and without arms available in the hall if sitting on the floor is not accessible for you.

The communal kitchen space has chairs with back support and without arms as well as bench style seating.

Can I come alone?

You are welcome to come alone, if you are especially nervous please speak to one of our rainbow lanyarded volunteers who will do their best to introduce you to people and help you feel welcome.

Will I be able to find a tying partner if I come without one?

Do not expect to tie with someone new to you the first time you attend RiS. We encourage everyone who attends RiS to practice self-tying whether you prefer to top or bottom for rope.

Our 101 sessions are designed to enable self-tying and we encourage everyone to participate in these as there is useful and transferrable information for both roles.

We have a Connections Board to facilitate getting to know people and finding those who share compatible rope interests but people often spend time self-tying and getting to know people their first few times at RiS.

Can I bring a friend who isn’t on fet?

You can! We’d prefer if you let us know in advance as you would be our first point of contact if any incidents occurred that needed to be dealt with after the event. We only ask that you share our Values and Code of Conduct with them before attending.

Can I come as a complete beginner?

Absolutely! We have all the basic equipment available to lend. There is also a Connections Board to help you meet people with similar goals and interests so just bring yourself and some enthusiasm.

Open Tying events are probably the least accessible for absolute beginners as most attendees will most likely be focused on their own tying plans, but we’re a friendly bunch and you won’t be turned away.

Our Peer Share sessions have more opportunity to connect with community members to show you the ropes and a learning corner with various books available.

If you’re looking for a more structured learning experience we’d recommend starting out at one of our quarterly 101 sessions which you should be able to find in our event listings.

Can I learn x skill at RiS?

Probably! Our community is varied and have many skills between us. This is primarily a peer sharing event and you will have to proactively seek out people who are able and willing to teach the skills you want to learn. We have a Connections Board to facilitate this and a channel in our Discord server.

We do run a quarterly 101 to get you up to speed with the basics and occasionally do one-off teaching events if there is someone who has an idea and a skill they want to share more formally.

Can I teach x skill at RiS?

Probably! Feel free to offer any skill share in our opening circle (just grab a rainbow lanyarded volunteer and let us know and we’ll let folks know), via our Connections Board or if you’d like to present a more formal class send us a DM on fet, DM one of the logistics team in our Discord or chat to a rainbow lanyarded volunteer about it in person.

What is the difference between RiS and a play event?

When we say we are not a play event the things we infer by that statement are:

  • the lights are brighter, music is more background level volume to allow for low level talking
  • this is an environment to expect people to chat, teach each other, work in small groups, or practicing tying 1-to-1, labing (practicing) techniques
  • this is an environment where vanilla clothing is required
  • this is an environment where being in a severely altered headspace is not appropriate
  • this an environment where aftercare space is not provided
  • there are no DM’s monitoring peoples safety during their tying
  • this is a rope specific space, other things such as impact which may be sometimes be included in rope play, are not allowed in the space

What is the difference between Peer Share and Open Tying events?

What to expect from each type of event is available in the event descriptions. On a basic level expect more chatting and groups working on self tying or learning skills together at Peer Share events and at Open Tying expect most socialising to happen in the kitchen area while people are more likely to be tying together in a more scene-adjacent way.
